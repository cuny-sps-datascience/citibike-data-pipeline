# Project synopsis and professor questions

October 27, 2026 · Briefing for the departure-feature preview

Our main project forecasts recorded Citi Bike departures for **three separate future hours at Manhattan stations**. We will test whether forecast weather and subway publication signals improve released demand-profile and calendar baselines. Inventory supports station display and snapshot collection; the 30-minute stockout experiment is optional work, not a requirement for the departure model.

We reused the existing trip archive to build and share a **689-station panel for March 2025–August 10, 2026**, containing 26.18 million station-origin-horizon rows. The new [preview](previews/departure_features/README.md) exposes one station's features, labels, crosswalk and time alignment across all three horizons, with a small animated map. It demonstrates proposed 24-hour-operation zeros and release-safe demand profiles. Those changes have **not yet replaced the published panel**. Making #58 independent of #57 is a concrete [separation plan](departure_feature_preview_plan.md), not a completed branch change.

| Likely question | Succinct answer |
|---|---|
| What exactly do you predict? | Recorded departures in `[T,T+1h)`, `[T+1h,T+2h)` and `[T+2h,T+3h)`. These are separate hourly counts, not cumulative totals or inventory forecasts. |
| Does this measure unmet demand or subway substitution? | No. Recorded trips reflect served demand and available supply. Alert associations are predictive; they do not establish that a disruption caused extra bike trips or that rebalancing would increase ridership. |
| Why fill missing departures with zero? | The proposed revision assumes continuous operation between first and last inferred active days, and requires complete trip files. It labels assumed zeros explicitly; outside that period, with incomplete files, or during ambiguous fall-back hours, counts remain unknown. This is not verified operation. |
| How do profiles avoid future information? | Both profile counts and inferred exposure use only release-eligible trips before the issue cutoff. In the sample, March/April releases supply the eight-week hour-of-week means. Source release dates currently follow an explicit, unverified month-end-plus-45-day assumption. |
| Why different timestamps for rain and temperature? | Temperature/wind are instantaneous at target start. Precipitation stamped 11:00 covers 10:00–11:00, so rain/snow accumulations use target end. The preview exposes both stamps and source-run availability. The published panel still needs this accumulated-variable correction. |
| Why would a 09:40 alert be missed at 10:00? | The old 09:00 aggregate was conservative but loses recent information. A 09:40 publication should qualify if available before 09:55. We plan an event-version rebuild at the actual cutoff; the 10:00 aggregate could include ineligible 09:57 updates and cannot simply be substituted. |
| Are all weather and station inputs historically operational? | No. Current-snapshot geography is retrospective, the seven-hour weather delay is assumed, and early weather archives have hindcast limitations. These are disclosed research assumptions, not proof that the exact inputs were issued then. |
| Have these new features improved accuracy? | Not established for the 689-station panel. An earlier Manhattan-total weather Poisson benchmark achieved retrospective MAE 761 departures per borough-hour. That is a different population and unit; it does not establish station-model or subway-feature performance. |
| Is the historical test an operational simulation? | No. Earlier periods have been inspected, and May validation outcomes were unavailable at the first June origin under our release assumption. Feature availability and model-fitting/selection availability require separate checks. |
| What does the animated map prove? | It helps inspect three actual departure-label hours, missing/assumed-zero states and mapped publication signals. It is not a fitted forecast, measured incident impact or a causal affected-area map. Origin-time alert inputs stay fixed while horizons advance. |
| What must happen before the main study is complete? | Version/rebuild the revised labels and profiles; obtain raw alert versions for current-cutoff features; perform common-key baseline/weather/subway comparisons with availability-aware fitting; confirm class-catalog delivery; evaluate live forecasts only after outcomes mature. |

The sample's observed departures are **17, 14, 18**; its released-profile means are **10.625, 13.375, 12.5**. The latter are historical means, not fitted-model predictions. Prepared rows, data checks and a working map are engineering progress, not predictive accuracy.
